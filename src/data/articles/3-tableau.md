## The Limitations of Spreadsheets

We've all been there: staring at an Excel spreadsheet with thousands of rows, trying to make sense of the data. While Excel is a powerful tool for data entry and basic manipulation, it falls short when you need to uncover hidden trends or present findings to non-technical stakeholders.

## Why Tableau?

Tableau bridges the gap between raw data and actionable insights. It allows you to drag and drop data fields to instantly create complex visualizations without writing a single line of code.

### My Workflow

* **Data Cleaning:** Before importing into Tableau, I ensure the data is clean and structured correctly. This often involves unpivoting columns and handling missing values.
* **Connecting Data:** Tableau can connect directly to Excel, SQL databases, or even cloud services like Google BigQuery.
* **Building the Dashboard:** I start by identifying the key metrics (KPIs) the stakeholders care about. Then, I use appropriate charts—bar charts for comparisons, line charts for trends over time, and maps for geographical data.

## Telling a Story

A good dashboard doesn't just show numbers; it tells a story. Use colors purposefully to highlight important information, and provide interactive filters so users can drill down into the data themselves. When stakeholders can interact with the data, they are much more likely to trust the insights.
